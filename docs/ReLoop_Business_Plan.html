Collect from the bazaar.
Sort at one hub. Resell what still works.

ReLoop buys used and broken electronics from Jammu's repair shops, retailers and institutions. It grades every item at one hub in the old-city market belt, then resells, repairs or strips it for parts. Only what is truly dead goes to an authorised recycler.

01 · Supply

We collect from the shops that already hold the stock

Repair shops and retailers sit in two streets and produce e-waste every week. They already know what works and what can be fixed. One pickup route covers 25 of them. Institutions add big lots a few times a year. Households give single items that cost more to collect than they're worth, so they come to us.

02 · Collection, storage, handling

Every item passes through one hub before it leaves

The hub is a rented ground-floor room of about 250 sq ft in the old-city market belt, between Raghunath Bazaar and Residency Road. Ground floor for heavy items and a rolling shutter for e-rickshaw loading. Budget ₹10,000 a month. We only need a room, not a warehouse: at 280 items a month and 75 days maximum stock, about 300 items are on the racks at any time.

AWorks, cleanPowers on, all functions pass the checklist. Cleaned, photographed, listed.
BOne fixable faultScreen, battery, port or keyboard. Partner shop repairs it for a fixed fee per job, then it is listed.
CNot worth fixingGood parts removed: screens, boards, RAM, batteries that test healthy, chargers, motors. Shell goes to D.
DDead or hazardousBagged by category, weighed, held in the scrap cage. Never opened beyond simple part removal.

Storage rules

  • Tag on arrival. Every item gets an ID like RL-JMU-0142 and a row in the inventory sheet: source, date, category, grade, buy price, photo.
  • Racks by grade. Steel slotted racks, off the floor. Shelf label matches the grade column in the sheet.
  • Batteries apart. Loose and swollen lithium cells go in a closed metal cabinet with sand. Never stacked or crushed. Out to the recycler every week.
  • Screens upright. Monitors and laptops stand on edge with card dividers.
  • Data wiped before listing. Factory reset plus overwrite for phones. Hard drives wiped, or drilled if they fail the wipe. Logged against the tag.
  • Room conditions. Dry, locked, CCTV on the shutter, one fire extinguisher near the bench.

04 · Money

We earn the gap between what we pay and what sorting unlocks

Buy prices are set as a share of what the item will resell for, so if resale prices fall, buy prices fall with them. Sellers choose cash on pickup at our price list, or consignment for items worth more than ₹10,000, where they get 70% when it sells.

05 · Compliance

Pilot through an authorised partner, then register ourselves

Under the E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022, refurbishers and recyclers must register on CPCB's EPR portal and hold consent and hazardous-waste authorisation. Bulk consumers such as offices and colleges may hand e-waste only to registered producers, recyclers or refurbishers.

Phase 1 · pilot
  • We trade in used equipment for reuse: working and repairable items bought from shops and people.
  • Every D-grade item and every battery goes only to VRG Groups, Gangyal, a JKPCC-authorised dismantler with a capacity of 135 tonnes a year. Each handover gets a weight receipt.
  • For institutions, their dead e-waste is handed directly to VRG with the institution named on the receipt. We buy only the working and repairable equipment from them.
Phase 2 · from month 6
  • Apply to JKPCC for consent to operate and hazardous-waste authorisation for the hub.
  • Register on the CPCB EPR portal as a refurbisher. Then institutions can hand their full lots to us directly.
  • Refurbishing certificates become a second income line once registration is in place.
